Artifact 1Working Prototype

FactoryAssist AI Reaction-Plan Assistant

A manufacturing decision-support prototype designed to make reaction-plan information easier to find when equipment problems occur.

View Live Demo →
View Code & Repository →

Problem & Objective

Operators and maintenance teams need clear, consistent guidance during equipment issues. The objective was to reduce confusion and make approved reaction-plan information easier to locate and understand.

Process

  1. Identified operator, maintenance, and manager needs.
  2. Defined the user flow and core features.
  3. Created a clickable front-end demonstration.
  4. Developed a Python/FastAPI back end with sample reaction-plan matching.
  5. Added safety guidance, escalation steps, sample equipment history, and management information.

Tools & Technology

HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Python, FastAPI, JSON, Git, GitHub, and AI-assisted development support.

Current Result

Produced a working prototype that demonstrates equipment selection, structured reaction plans, safety and escalation guidance, sample maintenance history, and a manager reliability dashboard. The live demonstration and source repository provide visible evidence of the current build.

Challenge

A key challenge was balancing fast, useful guidance with the need to preserve established safety procedures and qualified maintenance judgment. The design therefore separates informational support from situations that require escalation.

What I Learned

Successful AI-supported applications depend on more than the technology. Data quality, user experience, safety, explainability, and the needs of the people using the system all affect whether the solution creates value.

How I Would Measure Effectiveness

Response time to locate a reaction plan; downtime minutes per event; percentage of events using the system; escalation accuracy; repeat-failure rate; reaction-plan accuracy against approved procedures; and operator/maintenance satisfaction.

AI/ML Next Step

The current prototype uses structured matching rather than claiming an unvalidated predictive model. A future phase could evaluate historical downtime and maintenance data to determine whether a machine-learning model can reliably identify recurring failure patterns. Candidate models would be compared using appropriate validation metrics before deployment.

References

FastAPI Documentation • Project materials and sample data are available in the linked repository.

Artifact 2Early Development

Kids' Anxiety Educational AI Concept

An age-appropriate AI learning concept designed to help children recognize anxious feelings and practice appropriate coping strategies while preserving the role of trusted adults and qualified professionals.

View Project Repository →

Objective

Explore a supportive, easy-to-understand learning experience that helps children identify anxious feelings and practice appropriate coping skills.

Process

  1. Identified elementary-age children as the audience.
  2. Defined the need for developmentally appropriate language.
  3. Considered topic-based guidance and interactive learning.
  4. Created an initial GitHub repository.
  5. Identified safety, privacy, and adult-escalation considerations.

Current Result

Established the project concept, audience, responsible-use boundaries, and initial repository. This artifact demonstrates early-stage AI product thinking rather than presenting the concept as a completed clinical or therapeutic tool.

Challenge

The primary challenge is designing for a sensitive audience. Any automated guidance must use developmentally appropriate language, protect privacy, avoid diagnosis, and clearly direct children toward trusted adults when needed.

What I Learned

Responsible AI design begins before model selection. Audience, risk, privacy, escalation, and the limits of automation must shape the system requirements from the beginning.

How I Would Measure Effectiveness

Comprehension testing; age-appropriate response ratings; percentage of high-risk scenarios correctly escalated; unsafe-response rate; user satisfaction; and expert review of content accuracy and boundaries.

Tools & Technology

GitHub, AI-assisted concept development, human-centered design principles, responsible-AI planning, and professional experience supporting children.

References

NIMH: Children and Anxiety Disorders • NIST AI Risk Management Framework

Artifact 3Concept Development

Predictive-Maintenance AI Assistant

A decision-support concept for using maintenance and operating data to surface warning patterns and help teams prioritize equipment for closer inspection.

View Artifact Page →

Objective

Help maintenance employees recognize risks earlier and prioritize preventive action using operational evidence rather than relying only on reactive maintenance.

Data & ML Approach

Potential inputs include alarms, inspections, downtime records, cycle time, maintenance history, and sensor readings. The modeling phase would begin with data cleaning and baseline analysis, then compare appropriate predictive approaches. Model choice would be based on the business question, data quality, interpretability, and validated performance—not complexity alone.

Current Result

Defined the users, candidate data inputs, prototype workflow, safety considerations, and validation requirements needed before a predictive model should be trusted in an operational environment.

Challenge

Maintenance records can be incomplete, inconsistently categorized, or missing context. A predictive model trained on poor-quality records could produce misleading recommendations, making preprocessing and verification critical.

What I Learned

Predictive maintenance is as much a data-quality and change-management problem as a modeling problem. A model must be evaluated against a useful baseline and monitored after deployment for changing equipment conditions and data drift.

How I Would Measure Effectiveness

Precision and recall for failure-risk alerts; false-alarm rate; lead time before failure; unplanned downtime; repeat failures; preventive-to-reactive maintenance ratio; and avoided downtime hours. Model metrics would be reported only after actual training and validation.

Tools & Technology

Predictive-maintenance workflow design, data-analysis concepts, AI-assisted research, responsible-AI planning, and planned Python-based modeling.

References

U.S. DOE: Operations & Maintenance • NIST AI RMF

Artifact 4System Design Concept

Plant Emergency Resource-Locator System

A plant emergency-response concept using numbered facility columns as a shared location framework for quickly locating hazards and emergency resources.

View Artifact Page →

Objective

Improve emergency communication and help facilities and safety teams find the correct resources quickly using a location framework employees already understand.

Process

  1. Identified the existing column-number system as the shared reference.
  2. Defined emergency-resource categories.
  3. Outlined a searchable location and resource database.
  4. Considered map, QR-code, mobile, and dashboard interfaces.
  5. Identified controlled-access and verification needs.

Current Result

Produced a system-design concept connecting facility locations with emergency resources while identifying access-control and data-verification requirements.

Challenge

Emergency information must be accurate and current. Outdated locations or incorrect resource records could create risk, so ownership, verification frequency, and change control are essential parts of the design.

What I Learned

A strong technology solution should fit existing human workflows. Building around a location system employees already use can reduce learning burden, but the underlying data still requires disciplined governance.

How I Would Measure Effectiveness

Average resource-location time; percentage of resources with verified locations; search success rate; time since last verification; drill performance; user adoption; and user confidence during emergency-response exercises.

Tools & Technology

Process mapping, database planning, emergency-response workflow design, AI-assisted concept development, and planned dashboard/mapping technologies.

References

OSHA Emergency Preparedness • OSHA 1910.38 Emergency Action Plans
